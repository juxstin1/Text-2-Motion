The slow art of sourdough
Words by Inès Morel
B
read, at its best, is a negotiation with time. The baker mixes
flour and water, folds in a spoonful of yesterday’s starter,
then does the hardest thing in any kitchen: nothing at all.
Twelve hours later the dough has turned sour and alive, its gluten
stretched into a web that will hold the oven’s heat. No machine has
learned to hurry this part, and few bakers would want one to.
